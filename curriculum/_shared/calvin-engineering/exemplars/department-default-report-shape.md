# Formal lab report: department-default shape

Shape only. Use this for a longer formal lab report when the course follows the department-default choices: a separate abstract page, double spacing, and third person. Check the handout first. All content here is a stand-in and describes no real lab.

---

**Title page**

- Descriptive title, for example *Settling Time of a Stand-In Damped Oscillator*
- [Student Name]
- [Partner Name], if the course lists partners
- Course and section
- [Instructor]
- Date

**Abstract** (a page of its own)

One paragraph: why the work was done, what kinds of tests or models were used, and what they showed, in words rather than detailed numbers. Do not cite figures or tables.

**Body** (numbered sections; this is one reasonable choice, not a required list)

1. **Introduction.** What is being studied and why. Give the background a classmate would need.
2. **Theory.** Key relationships, typed and numbered, each explained in the sentence that uses it.
3. **Method.** A labelled schematic (Figure 1, caption beneath it). Then what was done, written in past tense as a report, not as instructions.
4. **Results.** Table 1 (caption above it), with units in the headings. Figure 2 compares measured points (markers only) against the model (a line only), readable in grayscale.
5. **Discussion.** Why the results look as they do. How far measurement and model disagree, as a percent difference, and the likely cause. What would reduce the disagreement.
6. **Conclusions.** One answer per objective, plus any follow-up work worth doing.

**Appendices** (each one pointed to from the body)

- A. Raw data
- B. One sample calculation, with units at each step
- C. Spreadsheet output, trimmed to sensible significant figures

---

Where the thermo-fluids courses differ, see `title-page-short-report-shape.md`: they put the abstract on the title page with numbers, use 1.5 spacing, and ban first person outright.
