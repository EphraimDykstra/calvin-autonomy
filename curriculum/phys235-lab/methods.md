## The deliverable

A short weekly write-up that follows the lab handout:

- A title block at the top of page 1 with the lab title, the experimenters (partners), and the date. There is no cover page.
- Answers numbered to match the handout's items, in order. When one answer covers several items, label it with all their numbers.
- Data tables and pasted images (scope traces, simulations, drawings, schematics) placed at the item that asks for them.
- Usually a short reflection at the end. Nothing in the archive says whether it is required.

The handouts are worksheets. They ask for predictions before building, drawings and schematics side by side, and observations compared against predictions, with instructor sign-off checkpoints between sections. The work is done in pairs.

## Methods

The recurring analytical moves are listed with steps and pitfalls in `pack.json`: predict, build, and compare; junction, loop, and power-balance checks on DC networks; scope-based amplitude, RMS, and reactance for AC circuits; and RC/RL time constants. The most common error in this material is **peak versus peak-to-peak**. Always state which quantity the instrument reported before converting it.

## When producing work for this lab

1. Get the handout for the specific week. Without it the item numbering, which is the whole structure, is unknown.
2. Answer every item. A write-up with skipped items is the failure mode seen in the archive.
3. Show the relation, the substitution, and the result with units for every quantitative item, even though no rule demands it.
4. Keep any reflection to the physics and to specific error sources.
